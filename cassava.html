CASSAVA(Manihot esculenta)

Cassava (Manihot esculenta), also known as yuca or manioc, is a woody shrub native to South America and widely cultivated across tropical regions as a primary staple crop.

Physical & Culinary Characteristics

  • Roots: Long, tapered tuberous roots with a rough, dark brown outer bark and firm
  • Foliage: A perennial woody shrub that grows 1 to 4 meters tall with large, palmately divided green
  • Flavor & Texture: Neutral, slightly nutty flavor with a dense, starchy texture
  • Toxicity Warning: Raw cassava contains naturally occurring cyanogenic glycosides, which are toxic.

    • Culinary Applications

  • Direct Cooking: Boiled, baked, or fried like potatoes
  • Flours & Starches: Ground into gluten-free cassava flour or processed into tapioca starch for baking
  • Snacks & Sweets: Used to produce cassava chips, cakes, puddings, and flatbreads

    • type of soil

  • Cassava grows best in light, well-drained, sandy loam soils
